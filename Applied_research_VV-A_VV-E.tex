\subsection*{Item VV-A1-09: Project Annotation}

The advent of quantum computers poses an imminent security threat to cooperative intelligent transport systems, making a transition to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) unavoidable. Standardized PQC signature schemes fit vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication poorly. ML-DSA (Dilithium) produces 2,420-byte signatures that can overload communication channels in dense traffic, while Falcon, with 666-byte signatures, saves bandwidth but its computationally demanding verification threatens the critical 10 ms latency limit at busy intersections. Existing evaluations rarely reflect realistic traffic, so it remains unclear which PQC scheme, if any, satisfies V2X constraints.

\par
The main objective of the V2X-PQC project is to systematically benchmark PQC signature algorithms under realistic V2X conditions and, depending on the findings, to design a new algorithm fit for this use case. To this end, the team will extend the open-source AWSIM simulator with a V2X communication layer and a message verification module. This will enable reproducible experiments with standardized BSM messages, varying traffic density, message rates of up to 1,000 messages per second, and the limited processing capacity of verifying devices.

\par
The project will deliver a simulation framework, a benchmark of candidate PQC schemes (including Dilithium and Falcon) measuring signature size, channel load, verification latency and throughput, and a research paper summarizing the results. Building on the identified bottlenecks, the project will also propose and evaluate an original PQC algorithm, or an optimization of an existing one, targeting verification latency below 10 ms at 1,000 messages per second and substantially less transmitted cryptographic data than Dilithium. The results will give vendors, operators and standardization bodies evidence-based guidance for adopting quantum-safe V2X security.

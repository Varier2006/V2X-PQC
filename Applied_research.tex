\section*{Application for Applied Research (Call VV-2024)}
\vspace{10pt}
\subsection*{Item VV-A1-03: Project Title}
\textbf{\large Implementation of Post-Quantum Cryptography in V2X Systems}
\vspace{15pt}
\subsection*{Item VV-A1-04: Project Acronym}
\textbf{\large V2X-PQC}
\vspace{15pt}
\subsection*{Item VV-A1-09: Project Annotation}

The advent of quantum computers poses an imminent security threat to modern cooperative intelligent transportation systems, making the transition to post-quantum cryptographic (PQC) standards necessary. Although the standardized ML-DSA (Dilithium) algorithm is widely applicable in the IT sector, it faces significant limitations in vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication. Its 2,420-byte digital signature can overload communication channels under high traffic density. The alternative Falcon algorithm, with a 666-byte signature, reduces bandwidth consumption but entails substantial computational costs for verification. At busy intersections, this places an excessive burden on roadside units (RSUs), causing the critical latency threshold of 10 ms to be exceeded.

\par
The main objective of the V2X-PQC project is to investigate and optimize PQC algorithms for V2X communication and develop a specialized software solution for roadside units (RSUs) and vehicle on-board units (OBUs). The proposed system will enable highly efficient verification of cryptographic signatures on messages shared among road users through PQC header compression and multithreaded processing of standardized Basic Safety Messages (BSMs).

\par
The key project outcome will be an IEEE 1609.2-compatible software solution that reduces the volume of transmitted cryptographic data by more than 40\% and guarantees verification latency below 10 ms even at a load of 1,000 messages per second. Through hardware-specific code optimization, the resulting software will achieve a throughput of over 1,200 PQC signatures per second and reduce latency to below 5 ms. The project will optimize existing security standards for connected vehicles and adapt them to current technical requirements in the automotive industry.
